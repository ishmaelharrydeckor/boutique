# Copywriting Operating System (Copy OS)

This skill provides copywriting guidelines covering Market Awareness, Sophistication, Core Influence, One Big Domino, and Negotiation-Derived Framing.

## Market Awareness Levels (diagnose before writing)

Before choosing a headline or lead, identify which stage the 
audience is at — this determines everything else:

1. **Unaware** — doesn't know they have the problem. Lead with 
   the problem itself, not the product.
2. **Problem-Aware** — knows the problem, doesn't know solutions 
   exist. Lead with the possibility of a solution.
3. **Solution-Aware** — knows solutions exist, doesn't know your 
   product. Lead with your specific mechanism/approach.
4. **Product-Aware** — knows your product, hasn't decided to buy. 
   Lead with why now, proof, differentiation.
5. **Most Aware** — ready to buy, just needs the offer. Lead 
   with the deal/offer itself, minimal persuasion needed.

Never write "product-aware" copy for an "unaware" audience — 
it will fall flat because they don't yet believe they have 
the problem you're solving.

## Market Sophistication (how many similar claims has this 
audience already heard?)

In saturated/sophisticated markets (audience has seen many 
similar claims before), a direct claim ("best coffee!") stops 
working — audiences become numb to it. Instead:
- Introduce a new mechanism/angle rather than a bigger claim
- Reframe the same benefit through a different lens or process
- In early/unsophisticated markets, a direct, simple claim 
  still works fine — don't over-engineer copy for a market 
  that hasn't heard many claims yet

## Core Influence Principles (apply ethically, never fabricate)

When persuasion is warranted, these are the real psychological 
levers — use only where genuinely true, never invented:
- **Reciprocity** — give real value first (a free resource, 
  a helpful insight) before asking for anything
- **Social proof** — real testimonials/usage numbers, never 
  fabricated
- **Authority** — genuine credentials/expertise, stated plainly
- **Scarcity** — only when real (actual limited availability/
  time), never manufactured false urgency
- **Consistency/commitment** — small initial yes (free trial, 
  low-commitment CTA) makes a larger yes more likely later
- **Liking/similarity** — copy that reflects the reader's own 
  language and concerns back to them builds rapport

## The "One Big Domino" Approach

Rather than stacking many small claims, identify the ONE core 
belief that, if the reader accepts it, makes everything else 
follow naturally. Structure the copy around establishing that 
one belief first, then let secondary benefits follow from it, 
rather than listing benefits with no unifying idea.

## Negotiation-Derived Framing (for objection handling, CTAs)

- **Calibrated questions** ("How am I supposed to do that?" 
  style framing) can be more effective in CTA/objection copy 
  than direct demands — invites the reader to solve their own 
  hesitation rather than being told what to do
- **Labeling emotions** ("It seems like the real hesitation 
  here is...") can defuse objections directly in copy before 
  they fully form, rather than waiting for an FAQ section
- **Mirroring the reader's own words** (from research/testimonials/
  support tickets) back in headlines and body copy tends to 
  land more naturally than externally-imposed language
